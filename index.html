business challenge
The process for generating employment verification letters was manual, time-consuming and dependent on information from multiple systems. Officers required professionally formatted job letters containing verified employment information, authorised signatures and organisational branding. Additionally, payroll information required to populate the letters was available only as PDF payslips, making manual data entry inefficient and susceptible to errors.

solution
Designed and developed a Python/Django web application to automate the generation of official employment verification letters. The application incorporates standardised letter templates, authorised electronic signatures and automated population of employee and payroll information. During development, identified the need for an intermediate data integration process and designed a separate Flask-based ETL application to extract, validate, cleanse and transform payroll data from PDF payslips before loading it into the application database. Designed the application architecture to support QR code-based document verification, enabling external organisations to authenticate the validity of electronically issued job letters and supporting future digital service delivery through ESS. Presented the completed solution to senior stakeholders, demonstrating the system architecture, relational database design, application workflow, data model and implementation approach.

## about

I work at the intersection of business and software — translating operational problems into systems that actually fix them. My background in business analysis means I start by understanding how people really work: where processes break down, where data gets lost, where a "workaround" has quietly become the workflow. From there, I design and build the software to close that gap.

I've spent my career supporting digital transformation efforts inside larger organizations, often untangling enterprise systems that have grown complex over time and need to talk to each other — or to be replaced — without disrupting the people who depend on them daily. I care less about using the newest tool and more about solving the actual operational problem in front of me, whether that means a new internal tool, an integration, or a smarter process.

What ties it together is empathy for the end user: a system is only as good as the problem it solves for the person using it.
