Attendance & Building Occupancy Management System

live

The Attendance & Building Occupancy Management System was developed in response to an elevated security concern within the Administration Building. At the time, the organisation had no effective means of determining who was present within the building during normal operations or emergency situations. Working as part of a committee appointed by the Director of Human Resources, I designed and implemented a cloud-based attendance and occupancy solution that evolved from a Google Sheets application into a Python-based system to support future scalability and enhanced functionality.

business challenge
During a period of heightened security concern, senior management identified the need for real-time visibility of building occupancy.
Although a biometric/key-card access system was considered, implementation was not possible due to infrastructure limitations.
Following this decision, the Sergeant responsible for the Guard Unit proposed a manual sign-in register for employees and visitors. The proposal was ultimately rejected because senior public servants were not required to sign upon arrival, making the process impractical and unlikely to achieve complete accountability.
A committee comprising representatives from the Guard Unit, Inspector Administration and Compliance and senior public servants from human resource, was therefore established to develop an alternative solution that could:
  • Account for everyone within the building in real time.
  • Monitor employee and visitor movements.
  • Operate without new infrastructure.
  • Be accessible during emergencies.
  • Be rapidly deployed.

  • Mobile accessibility.
  • Remote access for the Guard Unit.
  • Dashboard reporting.

This approach was selected because it could be implemented immediately without assistance from the IT Unit while remaining accessible during power outages and emergency situations.

Phase 2 – System Optimisation

Within the first week of implementation, the application exceeded Google's free Apps Script execution quota due to the volume of automated requests generated by the system.
Rather than treating this as a failure, I analysed the application's performance, redesigned several automated processes and reduced unnecessary requests while maintaining essential functionality.
This optimisation restored system stability and provided valuable insight into the scalability limitations of cloud-based scripting platforms.

Recognising the long-term limitations of the Google Sheets implementation, I designed Phase 1 of a Python-based application intended to provide:

The Python application represents the next stage in the system's evolution while preserving the operational workflows validated through the initial implementation.

Span of Control & Organisational Hierarchy Analysis

One of my key responsibilities during the Oracle Fusion implementation was supporting the development of the TTPS Span of Control, a comprehensive organisational hierarchy used to define reporting relationships and approval workflows within Oracle Fusion.
Unlike a conventional organisational chart, this exercise required constructing a hierarchical reporting structure from information gathered across police stations, divisions and specialised units throughout the organisation.
Business Challenge
Oracle Fusion requires clearly defined supervisory relationships to support automated approval workflows for processes such as leave, appointments and other Human Resource transactions.
Within the TTPS, however, organisational structures are highly dynamic. Officers are frequently transferred, reassigned or placed in acting appointments, and reporting relationships are not always centrally documented.
As a result, there was no single authoritative hierarchy that could be used directly within the system.

  • Validated reporting relationships with stakeholders.
  • Organised hierarchical reporting structures from frontline officers through successive supervisory levels.
  • Structured the information into a logical span of control suitable for Oracle Fusion configuration.
  • Worked with project stakeholders to refine organisational hierarchies where reporting relationships required clarification.
Contribution
The completed span of control provided a structured representation of supervisory relationships across the organisation and supported the configuration of Oracle Fusion approval workflows.
This work formed an important foundation for ensuring that system-generated approvals aligned as closely as possible with the operational management structure of the TTPS.

business challenge
To improve service delivery and reduce interruptions to operational departments, the Senior Superintendent of Human Resources established a centralized Customer Service Request (CSR) area within the building lobby. Customer Service Representatives (CSRs) would serve as the first point of contact for employee enquiries relating to Human Resources and Finance.
The initiative required an immediate mechanism for recording and tracking employee requests. However, implementation presented several operational constraints:

  • The solution needed to be operational within one working day to support the launch of the One Stop Shop.
  • Computers assigned to the CSR area had no network connectivity or Internet access, preventing the use of a centralized database or web application.
  • Despite operating independently, each workstation needed to generate unique ticket numbers while maintaining data consistency for future reporting and analysis.
A practical solution was therefore required that could be deployed immediately while providing a pathway to a more sophisticated Python application.

Retirement Management & Transition Tracking System (RMTS)


proposal & prototype

The Retirement Management & Transition Tracking System (RMTS) was conceived after several years of producing workforce analytics, retirement forecasts and executive reports within the iHRIS Unit. Through this work, I repeatedly identified gaps in the coordination of retirement activities across multiple departments, resulting in inconsistent visibility of officers approaching compulsory retirement.
Rather than continuing to report the symptoms, I developed a proposal and prototype for a centralized web-based application designed to manage the complete retirement lifecycle—from early identification through transition planning to retirement processing.
The proposal was developed to support the Trinidad and Tobago Police Service's proposed Retirement Transition Programme while strengthening collaboration between Human Resources, Finance and other operational stakeholders.

screenshots

business challenge
As part of my ongoing responsibility for retirement forecasting and workforce reporting, I regularly analysed employee data extracted from iHRIS and personnel systems to identify officers approaching compulsory retirement.
Through this analysis, I identified several recurring operational challenges:
  • Retirement information was distributed across multiple departments.
  • Officers on suspension, extended sick leave, secondment or temporary reassignment could be overlooked during retirement planning.
  • Departments operated independently with limited visibility into each other's activities.
  • Retirement activities were primarily reactive rather than proactively managed.
  • Critical pre-retirement tasks could be delayed due to fragmented information.
  • The absence of centralized tracking increased the risk of administrative delays and unnecessary salary overpayments.
These recurring issues demonstrated that reporting alone was insufficient to address the underlying business problem.
